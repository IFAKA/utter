How it works

Eight silent syllables.
Any word you want.

1

Wear it

A light band sits around your neck, with skin sensors under the jaw. A normal earbud does the talking.

2

Silently shape a syllable

Lips closed, no sound. Your tongue and jaw move slightly, as if you were about to say “ka”. The band reads those muscle signals.

3

Hear the word, and it's typed

The AI on your computer predicts the word you mean and whispers it. One more syllable accepts it, in any app you can type in.

Your mouth becomes an 8-key keyboard.

Four syllables narrow down the letters, like an old phone keypad. Four more steer. Most of the time the AI already knows the next word, so you just say ya.

Under the hood

It listens to muscles, not to sound.

Each syllable moves a different part of the tongue and jaw, so each leaves a different pattern across the sensors. A small model, trained on your own signals in a few minutes, tells them apart.

  • Sampling2,000 times a second

Questions.

Does it read my thoughts?

No. It only picks up deliberate muscle activity when you silently shape one of the eight syllables. Thinking a word without moving does nothing.

Can people tell I'm using it?

Your lips stay closed and there is no sound; your tongue and jaw move slightly. Proving that this works with lips closed is exactly the next milestone.

How fast is it?

In simulation, about 10 words a minute with a local language model. Most predicted words need just one syllable. That suits replies and agent prompts more than essays.

Which apps does it work with?

Anything you can type in: it types like a keyboard. With Claude Code it goes further, using the agent's messages as context and answering permission prompts.

Where does my data go?

From the band to your own computer over your Wi-Fi. Recognition and the language model run there, not in the cloud.

Is it a medical device?

No. It's a prototype input device, and it isn't designed or tested for medical use.
